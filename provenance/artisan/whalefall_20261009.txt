how do we live without harm?
how do we die so there is no vacuum?
how can we remain for others?

he named it whaleFall.

when a whale dies in the deep ocean, the carcass sinks
to the abyssal floor and becomes an ecosystem. hundreds
of species feed on it. some exist only on whale falls.
the bones last a century. the whale is more productive
in death than most organisms are in life.

the whale does not plan this. the architecture of the
whale — bone, fat, protein, mineral — determines what
can grow afterward. the contribution is structural,
not intentional.

this is the design problem for ecoPrimals.

if the VPS expires, does it leave a vacuum or a whale
fall? if the $6/month stops, does the ecosystem die
or does it feed the deep ocean?

AGPL is a whale fall decision. the code survives the
death of the server. executable science is a whale fall
decision. the tests run without golgiBody. published
methodology is a whale fall decision. K-NOME can be
replicated by anyone with domain expertise and an AI.

the antidote titration system is the first component
designed with whaleFall in mind. it heals the harm
automatically, without the maintainer, on a schedule.
if the server dies mid-titration, the tools are in git.

five requirements going forward:
1. net positive while alive
2. graceful in death
3. nutritive after death
4. autonomically healing
5. semantically compressible

the whale does not solve this problem.
the whale IS the solution.
the architecture of bone is the architecture of
generative death.

— the artisan. October 9, 2026.
